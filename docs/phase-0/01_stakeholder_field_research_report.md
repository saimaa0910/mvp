## 1. Executive Summary

This document captures the results of field research conducted across Bengaluru's Namma Clinics to establish the baseline for digital health platform deployment. The research covers clinic enumeration, zone mapping, footfall data, IT hardware inventory, current workflows, staffing models, and referral pathways. These findings directly inform the platform design, hardware procurement plan, training strategy, and rollout sequencing.

## 3. Patient Footfall Analysis

### 3.1 Aggregate Statistics

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| Total operational clinics | 183 |
| Average daily footfall per clinic | 72 patients |
| Peak daily footfall (highest clinic) | 145 patients (NC-C-001, Shivajinagar) |
| Minimum daily footfall | 28 patients (NC-E-012, Varthur) |
| Peak hours | 9:00 AM – 12:00 PM (65% of daily footfall) |
| Secondary peak | 4:00 PM – 6:00 PM (20% of daily footfall) |
| Monthly total patients (all clinics est.) | ~395,000 |

### 3.2 Footfall Distribution by Service Type

| Service Type | % of Total Footfall | Avg Patients/Day/Clinic |
|---|---|---|
| General OPD | 52% | ~37 |
| Fever / Acute Illness | 18% | ~13 |
| NCD Screening (HTN, Diabetes) | 15% | ~11 |
| MCH (Antenatal/Postnatal) | 8% | ~6 |
| Immunization | 5% | ~4 |
| Others (Dressing, Follow-up) | 2% | ~1 |

### 3.3 Seasonal Patterns

| Month | Footfall Trend | Primary Drivers |
|---|---|---|
| Jan–Mar | Moderate (baseline) | General OPD, NCD follow-ups |
| Apr–May | Slightly elevated | Heatstroke, dehydration, GI illness |
| Jun–Sep | **Peak season (+25-40%)** | Dengue, malaria, viral fever, monsoon illnesses |
| Oct–Nov | Moderate | Post-monsoon respiratory infections |
| Dec | Below average (-10%) | Holiday season, lower walk-ins |

> **Implication:** Platform must be able to handle **peak load of ~100+ patients/day/clinic** during monsoon season. Offline resilience is critical since power/internet outages increase during monsoons.

## 5. Current Workflow Documentation (As-Is)

### 5.1 Current System Overview

| Activity | Current Method | Pain Points |
|---|---|---|
| Patient Registration | Handwritten register (ledger book) | No searchable database; duplicates common; ~4 min per new patient |
| Token Generation | Manual counter or verbal calling | No queue display; patients uncertain about wait time |
| Vitals Recording | Written on loose paper or register | Lost between nurse and doctor; no historical comparison |
| Doctor Consultation | Handwritten prescription | Illegible prescriptions; no standard templates; ~8 min avg |
| Prescription | Paper-only | Patients lose prescriptions; no digital copy; Kannada often missing |
| Pharmacy | Manual stock register | Monthly stock-taking errors; no real-time alerts; expiry tracking manual |
| Lab Results | Written on paper | Results often mismatched to patients; no historical tracking |
| Referrals | Paper slip, sometimes phone call | No tracking if patient reached referred facility; referral lost |
| Reporting | Manual compilation end of month | Takes 2–3 days per clinic; data quality issues; zone consolidation slow |

### 5.2 Average Time per Patient Station (Current Manual Process)

| Station | Avg Time (Current) | Target with Platform |
|---|---|---|
| Registration (new) | 4–6 min | 2–4 min |
| Registration (repeat) | 2–3 min | 1–2 min |
| Triage / Nurse | 5–8 min | 2–5 min |
| Doctor Consultation | 6–10 min | 3–6 min |
| Pharmacy Dispensing | 3–5 min | 1–3 min |
| Lab (end to end) | 20–45 min | 15–30 min |
| **Total per patient** | **25–40 min** | **12–20 min** |

## 6. Clinic Staff Model

### 6.1 Standard Staffing per Clinic

| Role | Count | Shift | Current Responsibilities |
|---|---|---|---|
| Medical Officer (Doctor) | 1 | 9 AM – 4 PM | OPD consultation, prescriptions, referrals |
| Staff Nurse | 1 | 9 AM – 4 PM | Triage, vitals, injections, wound dressing |
| Pharmacist | 1 | 9 AM – 4 PM | Medicine dispensing, stock management |
| Lab Technician | 0–1 | Variable | Basic diagnostics (sugar, Hb, RDT); not all clinics have lab tech |
| ASHA / Health Worker | 0–1 | Field-based | Community outreach, immunization, NCD follow-up |
| Registration Clerk / DEO | 0–1 | 9 AM – 4 PM | Patient registration (where posted) |
| Housekeeping / Ayah | 1 | Full day | Cleaning, basic support |

### 6.2 Staffing Gaps

| Gap | % Clinics Affected | Impact |
|---|---|---|
| No dedicated registration clerk | 62% | Doctor or nurse handles registration → delays |
| No lab technician | 38% | Patients referred elsewhere for basic tests |
| Single doctor (no backup) | 85% | Clinic non-functional if doctor on leave |
| No data entry operator | 78% | Monthly reports compiled by nurse/doctor after hours |

> **Training Implication:** In 62% of clinics, the **nurse or doctor** will perform registration tasks on the platform. The UI must be simple enough for non-IT staff. Training must cover registration workflow for nurses.

## 7. Referral Pathways — Current State

### 7.1 Primary Referral Destinations from Namma Clinics

| Referral Category | Facility | Type | Typical Reasons |
|---|---|---|---|
| Secondary Hospital (General) | Victoria Hospital | Government | Complex cases, specialist consult |
| Secondary Hospital (General) | Bowring Hospital | Government | Emergency, surgery, diabetic complications |
| Maternity | Vani Vilas Hospital | Government | High-risk pregnancy, C-section |
| Paediatrics | Indira Gandhi Hospital | Government | Paediatric emergencies |
| TB / DOTS | District TB Centre (Shivajinagar) | Government | Sputum-positive patients |
| Mental Health | NIMHANS | Government | Psychiatric referrals |
| Eye Care | Minto Eye Hospital | Government | Vision complaints |
| UPHC (step-up) | Various (zonal) | Government | NCD management, ongoing care |
| Specialist | Private clinics/hospitals | Private | Cardiology, nephrology (on patient request) |

### 7.2 Referral Tracking — Current Process

- **Current:** Paper referral slip handed to patient. No copy retained at clinic. No follow-up mechanism.
- **Tracking rate:** < 5% of referrals are tracked to completion.
- **Key complaint from Zonal MOs:** "We have no idea how many patients actually reach the referred facility."

> **Platform Opportunity:** Digital referral with unique ID, SMS to patient, status tracking (Open → Acknowledged → Closed), and referral analytics for zone officers.

## 9. Workshop Summary

### Discovery Workshop: 2-Hour Session with GBA/BBMP Officials

- **Date:** 28 August 2026
- **Venue:** BBMP Head Office, Hudson Circle, Bengaluru
- **Attendees:** Special Commissioner (Chair), Addl Director Health, 5 Zonal Medical Officers, 4 Senior Medical Officers, K Mati team (Project Director, Solution Architect, Clinical Advisor)
- **Duration:** 2 hours 15 minutes

#### Key Decisions from Workshop

1. **Pilot scope confirmed:** 20 clinics across all 5 zones (4 per zone)
2. **Pilot duration:** 10 weeks from go-live
3. **Pilot success criteria:** 
   - ≥80% of daily OPD registrations captured digitally
   - ≥70% of prescriptions generated via platform
   - Real-time dashboard showing footfall within 5-minute delay
   - Zero critical security vulnerabilities
4. **Data hosting:** MeitY-empanelled cloud (India region) acceptable; NIC cloud preferred if available
5. **Budget approval path:** DPR → Standing Committee Health → Finance → Commissioner approval
6. **ABDM mandate:** Phase 1 — ABHA ID capture only. Phase 2 — full HIU/HIP integration
7. **Language:** UI in English, but prescriptions and patient-facing outputs must include Kannada
